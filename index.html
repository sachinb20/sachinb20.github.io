Sachin Bhadang

I'm a graduate student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at UC San Diego, where I work with Prof. Xiaolong Wang. My research focuses on contact-rich robotic manipulation and real-world adaptation for locomotion and dexterous manipulation.

Previously, I completed my undergraduate studies at IIT Kanpur, double majoring in Mechanical and Electrical Engineering. There, I worked with Prof. Laxmidhar Behera and Prof. Tushar Sandhan on robotic grasping in cluttered environments.

I was also a research intern at the PEARL Lab at TU Darmstadt, working with Prof. Georgia Chalvatzaki on scene-graph representations for interactive object search using mobile manipulators. Additionally, I worked as a robotics engineer at Kikobot, developing motor-control firmware and robot learning pipelines for a 6-DoF robotic arm.

Interactive Exploration and Scene Graphs for Hidden Object Search

Research internship · PEARL Lab, TU Darmstadt · Advisor: Prof. Georgia Chalvatzaki

Action-conditioned scene graphs and CNN-GRU PPO exploration policies in AI2-Thor that open, move and look behind things to reveal occluded objects, with an LLM-based planner that detects subtask failures from visual feedback and replans. Validated in simulation and on a Tiago++ mobile manipulator.

code: exploration code: scene graphs report

SmolVAM: Lightweight Video-Action Models for Robot Control

Project · UC San Diego

A lightweight video-action model that pairs a 0.7B Hummingbird video model and VideoMAE spatiotemporal features with a transformer flow-matching action decoder. LoRA fine-tuned on 20 MimicGen task variations; 60% success on cube stacking. Left: simulated rollout; right: frames imagined by the video model.

code report

IMU Orientation Tracking & Panorama Reconstruction

Course project · ECE 276A, UC San Diego

Quaternion-based orientation tracking with projected and geodesic gradient descent, fusing a gyroscope motion model with accelerometer gravity observations; motion-model initialization cut optimization iterations by ~64%. The estimated orientations are used to stitch spherical panoramas from camera frames.
